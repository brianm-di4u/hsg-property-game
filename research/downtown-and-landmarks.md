# Downtown Hot Springs and Landmarks: Central Avenue, Hotels, Clubs, Towers, Neighborhoods, Transport, Utilities and Money Values

Central Avenue (Valley Street until the late 1870s) runs through a narrow valley with Bathhouse Row on its east side and 1880s–1930 commercial blocks on its west, anchored at the north "Y" by the 1924 Arlington Hotel. This file covers the hotels (three Arlingtons, the lost Majestic and Eastman, the 2023–2026 Park Hotel rehab), the gambling-era club buildings, the three mountain towers, tall buildings and museums, restaurants, the Park Avenue motor courts and the Pleasant Street / Malvern Avenue "Black Broadway," civic and medical buildings, transport and utility history, and a master table of historical money values. Coordinates come from the NRHP list unless marked est.
Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Central Avenue and the historic district

- In the late 1870s Valley Street was renamed Central Avenue. Few substantial buildings went up before the 1876 title ruling. District buildings date from 1886 to 1930 — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/).
- The town incorporated Jan. 10, 1851, with two rows of hotels and bathhouses — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). conflict: Wikipedia's history section gives April 5, 1876 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- The Supreme Court ruled in 1876 that land title belonged to the federal government. The townsite was 196 blocks and about 50 miles of streets, and the reservation 264.93 acres — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Hot Springs Central Avenue Historic District:** Central Ave from Prospect to Park Sts. plus 101 Park Ave. NRHP June 25, 1985, boundary increase 2007, decrease 2019. Point 34.514722, -93.054444 — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas). 77 contributing properties, including the 1924 Arlington, the 1929 Medical Arts Building and the 1927 Wade Building — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- 2014 `modern`: nearly all of downtown went on the state's endangered-places list (May 28), and the American Planning Association named Central Avenue a "Great Street" (Oct. 1) — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/).
- Geology: the "alpha" thrust fault that brings spring water up roughly follows Central Avenue and Fountain Street — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm). Hot Springs Creek runs in a tunnel under Central Ave. (enclosed 1882–83) — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, AR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm).
- **Fires** (downtown-shaping): 1878 (March 5; eight hours; over 1,000 homeless — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); about 150 buildings, about $300,000 losses — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/); conflict: "almost 100 buildings, including the major portion of the business district" — [HMdb marker (via search summary)](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=211170)); 1905 (Feb. 25, began ~3 a.m. at the Grand Central Hotel, Chapel & Crown streets; up to 25 died; more than 100 acres and up to 400 structures, about $2 million; Grand Central and Moody hotels, synagogue and courthouse lost — [EOA Fire of 1905](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1905-12570); conflict: 2 dead and about 500 homeless — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/)); 1913 (Sept. 5, $10M; conflict: 20 acres (EOA fire entry) / about 50 blocks (ADG, NPS, EoA Crystal) / 60 blocks (EOA county, Wikipedia); 2,500+ homeless; at least 700 homes — [EOA Fire of 1913](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1913-14306); [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/)). After 1913, wood-shingle roofs were outlawed and the fire department doubled. July 2001 fire gutted two downtown buildings including the Ohio Club — [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2001/jul/12/historic-gambling-spot-gutted-by-fire/).
- **Floods:** May 1923 (water swept the Central Avenue business district, tossing cars and blowing manholes into the air — [1923 wire report](https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/sn84024326/1923-05-17/ed-1/seq-5.pdf)); May 1990 (~13 in.; shops across from the Row took several feet of water — [NPS Morning Report](https://npshistory.com/morningreport/incidents/hosp.htm)); May 19–20, 2020 (two waves covered Central Avenue, floated cars, ~85 rescued — [KARK (via search summary)](https://digital-release.kark.com/?p=352188)). No source for a notable 1997 flood.
- Visual: 2–4 story brick storefronts **[inferred]**; Victorian/Italianate commercial blocks not researched.

### Arlington Hotel
- **1st (1875):** financed by Samuel W. Fordyce and two partners (Arlington site: "Fordyce and others"); opened 1875 as the state's largest hotel, the same year as the Malvern narrow-gauge railroad (Wikipedia) — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/); [Wikipedia: Arlington Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)); [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **2nd (1892–93):** Spanish Renaissance, 300 rooms, four stories of red brick with corner towers. Burned April 5, 1923: one fireman died, about $1.6M damage. Detective William Pinkerton smoked a cigar on the veranda and lost all his belongings — [Wikipedia: Arlington Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)); [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **3rd (1924):** begun Oct. 25, 1923 off federal land, completed Nov. 28, 1924 in 11 months and 3 days. Amenities: an Arthur Murray dance studio, roof gardens, a bathhouse with private elevator, a gas station, a 10-piece Meyer-Davis orchestra and the Fountain Room — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). Architect George Mann — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/). Its "huge size... and placement at the terminus of the town's most important vista made the building a key Hot Springs landmark" — [Wikipedia, Arlington Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)). Note the user brief said 1925; Wikipedia says 1924.
- **Guests:** Al Capone (rented an entire floor in the 1930s), Babe Ruth, FDR, Truman, G.H.W. Bush and Bill Clinton — [Wikipedia: Arlington Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)). Clinton attended his high-school prom at the Arlington — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). John McGraw's arrest is marked at the Arlington ("near the Arlington," trail) — [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/).
- **Capone room** **[DISPUTED]**: Capone rented the fourth floor with Room 443 as his suite; a clerk once insisted he wear a tie to dinner — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). Room 443, now the "Al Capone Suite" — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/before-vegas-hot-springs-americas-sin-city-arkansas-spa-town-embraces-colorful-seedy-history-180989288/). NBC gives room 442 — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna24165026). The landmarks notes found "no source" for 443 in Wikipedia. Tunnels, possibly to the Southern Club (now the Wax Museum), are rumored **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **1928:** Sen. Joe T. Robinson accepted the Democratic vice-presidential nomination on the front steps — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **KTHS radio:** built 1924 on the upper floor of the new Arlington; Class B station authorized Dec. 19, 1924; test broadcasts from Dec. 11; first regular broadcast Dec. 20, 1924, 8:30 p.m., from the ballroom; mostly live big-band music; Chamber of Commerce took over May 27, 1928 — [EOA, KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/); [Arkansas Democrat-Gazette 2008](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2008/mar/09/remembering-arkansas-hot-springs-kths-was-20080309/). The Arlington history page says KTHS, the first radio station, broadcast from the hotel in 1928 — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **WWII `roaring`:** the Arlington and Majestic housed overflow soldiers from the Army-Navy Hospital — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). By Labor Day 1944 the Army had commandeered five hotels for a recreation and redistribution center handling about 3,000 servicemen a day "until the end of 1946" (conflict with the December 1945 closure — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas)). The Fountain Room became the officers' mess, the main dining room a mess hall and the ballroom a movie theater — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **1937 rate:** $4.50 a day (Park Hotel $3) per a Truman letter — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/).
- **1987:** Arlington Exhibit Center (May) — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **Ownership / restoration `modern`:** bought 2017 by Al Rajabi's Sky Capital Group of San Antonio; turned 100 on Nov. 28, 2024 — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/). Owner Al Rajabi / "Sky Capitol Group"; under renovation as of Aug 2023, restoring the facade to its 1924 look — [Wikipedia: Arlington Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)). A $30M renovation began 2023 and was finishing 2025; seven layers of stucco stripped to the brick; work included the roof and cooling tower — [EOA Arlington (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arlington-hotel-2987/); [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **2026:** booking sites show 2026 stays (reviews July 2026), about 470–478 units, pool closed Dec 18, 2025–Mar 6, 2026, nightly rates about $119–$190 — [Hotels.com [promo]](https://www.hotels.com/ho39732096/arlington-resort-hotel-and-spa-hot-springs-united-states-of-america/); [HotelPlanner [promo]](https://www.hotelplanner.com/Hotels/182327/Reservations-Arlington-Resort-Hotel-Spa-Hot-Springs-239-Central-Ave-71901?gencall=1).

### Majestic Hotel
- Built 1882 as the Avenue Hotel, renamed 1888 — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/). The Manning family owned it 1929–2006 — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/). Shuttled guests to the bathhouses every five minutes; Army redistribution center for about 18 months from 1944 — [Abandoned Arkansas (via search summary)](https://abandonedar.com/the-majestic-hotel/). Estella Hughey was an early Black masseuse there (1926) — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm). Trail marker: "1902, expanded 1926" — [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/).
- Parts closed in the 1980s; closed 2006 — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/).
- Fire Feb. 27, 2014 destroyed the yellow-brick section; firefighters fought it nearly 20 hours; no one injured; burned section razed within days in a roughly 12-hour operation; city board voted June 21, 2016 to demolish the rest; remaining condemned portion came down late 2016 — [EoA: Majestic Hotel](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/majestic-hotel-8351/); [EoA This Day Feb 27, 2014](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/this-day-ar-history/february-27-2014/); [EoA This Day June 21, 2016](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/this-day-ar-history/june-21-2016/); [NWA Online, Mar 1 2014](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2014/mar/01/burned-hotel-building-being-razed-20140301/). City acquired it 2015 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- Site: June 2021 board voted to sell to R.A. Wilson Enterprises for an outdoor music venue; buyer withdrew July 2022; as of 2024 an empty lot marketed as "Majestic Village" (hotel, condos, retail) — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); [THV11 (via search summary)](https://www.thv11.com/article/news/local/majestic-village-renderings-presented-hot-springs-board/91-6a438a78-41d2-4acb-bcbb-5ea099059244).
- Gaps: wing build dates, architect and street address not retrieved.

## Tall buildings and commercial blocks

- **Medical Arts Building**, 236 Central Ave (34.516389, -93.053889; NRHP 1978): 16-story Art Deco by Almand & Stuck of Little Rock, begun Dec. 1, 1929 on the old Rector Bath House site at a cost of $375,000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/). ### Pleasant Street "Black Broadway" and Malvern Avenue (African American history)
- **Pleasant Street HD** (NRHP 2003; 34.507222, -93.047778), the "Black Broadway," with 93 contributing buildings including Visitors Chapel AME (319 Church St., NRHP 1995) and the Woodmen of Union Building — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas); [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Antebellum:** 1860, Hot Spring County had 613 enslaved of 5,635 residents; many worked in hotels — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- **Schools:** Hot Springs Normal and Industrial Institute (Mebane Academy) for Black students, 1890s into the 1920s — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Langston High School served African American students from 1913; Hot Springs delayed integration more than a decade after *Brown* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)). School desegregation began 1956 with a high-school auto-mechanics course — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- **Bathhouses:** Crystal Bathhouse, 415 Malvern Ave (1904, John McCaslin; first exclusively for African Americans; Knights of Pythias lease 1908; burned 1913). Pythian Bathhouse & Sanitarium, same site (W.T. Bailey of Tuskegee; opened Dec. 27, 1914; half-price for Pythian insurance members; enlarged 1923; closed 1974; demolished) — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EoA: Crystal Bathhouse](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/crystal-bathhouse-4253/); [LOC HABS AR-41](https://www.loc.gov/item/ar0041); [Green Book project](https://community.village.virginia.edu/greenbooks/content/pythian-baths).
- **Woodmen of Union Building / National Baptist Hotel**, 501 Malvern Ave (34.506944, -93.049444): designed 1922 by W.T. Bailey, built 1923–24 for the Supreme Lodge of the Woodmen of Union, an African American fraternal group; four-story Classical Revival; held a hospital and nurse school, a bath hotel, a bank (vault survives), a theater and a print shop; hosted Count Basie, Peg Leg Bates and Joe Louis; closed 1935 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [Wikipedia Woodmen of Union Building (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_Union_Building); [Wikipedia: Woodmen of Union Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_Union_Building). 100-bed hospital, 75-room bath hotel and 2,500-seat auditorium — [Preserve Arkansas (via search summary)](https://preservearkansas.org/woodmen-of-union-building-national-baptist-hotel/); conflict: 2,000-seat theater plus 600-capacity auditorium (NPS) / 2,000 (Bathhouse Row article). Bought by the National Baptist Convention 1948 (NPS) conflict: 1950 by the National Baptist Association (Wikipedia, Preserve Arkansas); closed 1983 (NPS) conflict: 1981 (Preserve Arkansas); the manager said, "It's a small price to pay for integration"; senior apartments 2011 (snippet). NRHP June 27, 1997. Trail marker: "National Baptist Hotel (1923) became a center of African American culture" — [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/).
- **First Black masseurs/masseuses:** James Truman (Arlington, 1922) and Estella Hughey (Majestic, 1926) — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- **1913 fire origin:** Friday Sept. 5, just before 2:15 p.m., in Ulma Madlock's Church Street home when her ironing ignited a curtain (ADG: a charcoal stove heating irons at a Black-owned home laundry); city treasurer George Housley's men dynamited the People's Laundry at 356 Malvern Ave. without slowing the blaze — [EOA Fire of 1913](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1913-14306); [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/); [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- **Racial violence (sensitive; include with care):** Will Norman lynched June 19, 1913 before an estimated 5,000; Gilbert Harris lynched 1922; Oscar Chitwood murdered at the courthouse Dec. 26, 1910 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). 1919 "Little Georgia" nightriding case (Housley v. State) — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). 1941 Mitchell v. United States: Rep. Sam Guinn Field (1933, 497 Crescent Ave.) hosted Negro league spring training — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Johnson_Park).
- Other Malvern Ave houses: Doherty House (705 Malvern, NRHP 1978), W. H. Moore House (906 Malvern, NRHP 1990).

## Civic, medical and religious buildings

### Army and Navy Hospital / Arkansas Career Training Institute
- 105–425 Reserve St., 34.512022, -93.050383, NRHP 2007 — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas).
- 1882: at a dinner on the second floor of the Palace Bathhouse, ex-Confederate surgeon Dr. A.S. Garnett hosted Sen. John A. Logan, a former Union general, who promised funding. Late June 1882 Congress approved $100,000 for a 30-bed joint hospital, the first in U.S. history; President Chester A. Arthur signed it. Opened January 1887 under the secretary of war — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). conflict: 100 beds — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- $1.5M after WWI for a 500-patient hospital; six-story centerpiece from 1933 (Wikipedia) conflict: seven stories, fireproof brick and steel, about $1.5 million, completed 1933 (EOA) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). Upgrading Memorial Field's grass runways to concrete was tied to the 1930s hospital construction — [City of Hot Springs: Airport History](https://www.hotspringsar.gov/476/Airport-History).
- 1887–WWII: more than 100,000 patients. WWII: the Army's leading arthritis center and the largest U.S. center for adult polio; government bought the Eastman Hotel and connected it by a ramp, roughly tripling beds; Arlington and Majestic housed overflow soldiers — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). Joe DiMaggio was a patient — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Late 1940s–early 1950s: one of Garland County's top five employers; 1957 U.S. Army took control — [EOA ANH](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236).
- April 1, 1960: passed to the state (sold for $1 per Wikipedia) as the Hot Springs Rehabilitation Center, one of nine in the nation and the only one west of the Mississippi; renamed Arkansas Career Training Institute 2009; state announced closure May 2019 (Act 910 reorganization); Preserve Arkansas most-endangered list 2020; operations stopped 2020; empty since — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [UALR Public Radio / EoA Minute](https://www.ualrpublicradio.org/2023-06-12/encyclopedia-of-arkansas-minute-army-navy-hospital); [Preserve Arkansas](https://preservearkansas.org/army-navy-hospital/). Sept 2024: $2.75M federal grant for security and fire protection — [Rep. Westerman press release](https://westerman.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/westerman-sanders-applaud-army-navy-hospital-security-and-fire).
- WWII redistribution: military took the Eastman 1942; August 1944 the Army took over most hotels for a redistribution center that processed 32,000+ before closing December 1945 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Official Army name not found.
