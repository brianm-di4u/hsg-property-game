# Gangster Era and Gambling in Hot Springs

Hot Springs ran an illegal but openly tolerated gambling economy from the 1880s feuds to the State Police raids of 1967, with a peak under Mayor Leo P. McLaughlin (1927–1947), when casinos paid regular "fines" that worked as license fees and New York and Chicago mobsters vacationed or retired in town.
The 1946 GI Revolt broke the machine; gambling returned after Gov. Orval Faubus's 1954 election (the Vapors opened 1960); Gov. Winthrop Rockefeller's raids ended it in 1967, leaving Oaklawn as the only legal wagering until casino gaming arrived there in 2019.
This file covers the feuds, the machine, clubs, figures, the GI Revolt, the Vapors and the shutdown, plus today's museums. Facts marked "(via search summary)" or `(snippet)` came from search summaries. Use real names only in factual trivia/text cards; living people are never playable caricatures.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Overview

- **Two economies.** The legal economy was federally regulated bath prices, hotels and (intermittently legal) horse racing; the underground economy was casinos, wire rooms, bookies and slot routes, paying "fines" each period instead of license fees — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas) (framing is history.md's inference).
- 1927–1947 was the peak gambling period, with at least 10 major casinos — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). *Variety* (1959) described the casinos paying weekly fines and then reopening — same.
- In 1961 a federal probe called Hot Springs the largest illegal gambling operation in the U.S. — [EOA Madden](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/owen-vincent-madden-1702/).
- 1950–1965 brought a major hotel and motel building boom tied to casino gambling; the crackdown later hurt the business — [Rex Nelson, "Along Park Avenue"](https://www.rexnelsonsouthernfried.com/post/along-park-avenue).
- After the 1967 shutdown, Oaklawn remained the only legal gambling until other forms became legal about four decades later — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Smithsonian ran a feature calling Hot Springs America's "sin city" before Vegas — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/before-vegas-hot-springs-americas-sin-city-arkansas-spa-town-embraces-colorful-seedy-history-180989288/). David Hill's book *The Vapors* (2020) is the main narrative account — [America's Best Racing](https://www.americasbestracing.net/lifestyle/2020-the-vapors-explores-complicated-history-illegal-gambling-hot-springs).

## 19th-century gambling feuds

- **Flynn–Doran War.** In 1882 Frank Flynn wrecked two gambling clubs run by Jim K. Lane. In February 1884 Doran's men ambushed the Flynn brothers, killing a buggy driver and two gamblers and wounding Frank Flynn — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). EOA dates the shootout Feb. 9, 1884 (the Flynn–Doran War) — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). The landmarks notes summarize "Flynn–Doran feud in the 1880s, with the Feb 1884 ambush" — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Hot Springs Gunfight, March 16, 1899.** City police and the Garland County Sheriff's Office shot it out over control of gambling; three officers died — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- 1884: Superintendent Samuel Hamblen nearly shot on duty ("The Almost Shoot Out of 1884") — [NPS Stories](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/stories.htm) (motive not stated).
- Racing ties: Sportsman's Park (early 1890s), anti-gambling laws repealed 1903, Essex Park (1904; burned 1917 the day after reopening), Oaklawn (1904 Jockey Club formed by Southern Club owners); 1907 anti-betting ban — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/).
- Ballplayers gambled at the Ohio and Southern clubs (Baseball Trail marker "Ohio and Southern Clubs") — [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/).

## The McLaughlin machine (1927–1947)

- **Leo P. McLaughlin:** born June 5, 1888 in Hot Springs; class president and star athlete (Hot Springs High School, 1908); state legislator (1910); city attorney (1911, for 13 years); drafted May 27, 1918, discharged as a corporal April 26, 1919; ran in 1926 on an "open town" platform plus street paving; mayor 1927–1947, with only one challenger in all that time — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/).
- **Image:** rode down Central Avenue daily in a sulky pulled by horses "Scotch and Soda," in riding clothes with a red carnation — same.
- **Methods:** city employees got pink slips naming favored candidates; many supporters on the voter lists "were found only in cemeteries"; gamblers paid fines that worked as license fees — same. Partner: Municipal Judge Vern Ledgerwood — [EOA Madden](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/owen-vincent-madden-1702/).
- **Racing:** arranged Oaklawn's 1934 reopening after a 15-year gap — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/); with a group that reopened it without legislative consent March 1, 1934 — [EOA-OAK](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=4521).
- **Personal:** marriages to Juanita Gilliam (1918), Mary Francis Frink (1931, a few months) and Florence Paul (Nov. 4, 1931, the day after the second marriage ended; divorced 1936) — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/).
- **Fall:** March 1947 grand jury investigation; he did not run again; indicted for bribery, illegal voting and misuse of funds; never convicted; died May 5, 1958 — same. The airport, dedicated 1946 as McLaughlin Field, was renamed Hot Springs Memorial Field in May 1947 — [City: Airport History](https://www.hotspringsar.gov/476/Airport-History).
- **Violence/corruption context:** 1937 murder of an ex-sheriff who tried to enforce anti-gambling laws, no one charged — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). A reader comment on the EOA McLaughlin page says votes from people "dead for twenty years" flipped the 1942 assessor race (anecdotal, **[UNVERIFIED]**) — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/).
- Other sources: [Arkadelphian history minute](https://arkadelphian.com/2024/08/20/history-minute-leo-mclaughlin-and-the-fall-of-hot-springs-corruption/).

## The GI Revolt (1946)

- 1942: a federal grand jury found Garland County officials ignored most election laws — [EOA GI Revolt](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/GI-Revolt-4157).
- 1946: Sid McMath ran for prosecuting attorney of the 18th Judicial District (Garland and Montgomery counties). The machine used poll-tax receipts to beat every GI candidate in the primary except McMath. Pat Mullis sued. Receipts had been issued in alphabetical blocks of 6, 12 or 18 names in one handwriting (confirmed by a Kansas Bureau of Investigation expert), and Judge John Miller threw out almost 24% of the primary poll-tax receipts. In November 1946 the GI ticket won every Garland County seat — [EOA GI Revolt](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/GI-Revolt-4157).
- Second count: Judge Miller threw out **1,607** fraudulent poll-tax receipts, and every GI candidate for local office won — [Wikipedia: Sid McMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid_McMath). (The 24% share and the 1,607 count are from different sources; both may describe the same ruling.)
- Aftermath: Earl T. Ricks replaced McLaughlin as mayor; I.G. Brown sheriff (1947–49) — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). McMath won the governorship in 1948 and served 1949–1953; a 1947 grand jury indicted owners including McLaughlin, and most were acquitted — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). The reformers shut the casinos after 1946 — [Wikipedia: Sid McMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid_McMath).
- Almost half of the 1946 GI officeholders were criticized within a decade for using their offices for personal gain — [EOA GI Revolt](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/GI-Revolt-4157). Q. Byrum Hurst began as a GI reformer and later defended gambling — [EOA Quincy Byrum Hurst](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/quincy-byrum-hurst-7408/).

## Revival under Faubus and The Vapors

- After Faubus's 1954 election, casino gambling resumed — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). People notes date the revival 1955–67 — [Wikipedia: Sid McMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid_McMath); [EOA Quincy Hurst](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/quincy-byrum-hurst-7408/).
- Three big casinos of the 1950s–60s; the Southern Club the oldest — [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/).
- **The Vapors** (315 Park Ave.): built summer 1959 on the former Phillips Drive-In site by Dane Harris with Madden (and Harry Hastings); opened 1960 with a coffee shop, the Monte Carlo Room, a theater restaurant with retractable stage and a late-night casino. Tony Bennett first sang "I Left My Heart in San Francisco" there in rehearsal. Bombing Jan. 4, 1963: 12 injured (3 hospitalized), a 30-ft hole in the roof, and the gaming tables exposed in an Arkansas Gazette front-page photo; follow-up bombs hit Harris's home, Judge Plummer Dobbs's porch and prosecutor David Whittington's car — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/); [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/). Tiffany chandeliers, mahogany bar — [America's Best Racing](https://www.americasbestracing.net/lifestyle/2020-the-vapors-explores-complicated-history-illegal-gambling-hot-springs); [NWA / Rex Nelson (snippet)](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2020/jul/12/the-vapors/).
- **Belvedere** bombed March 11, 1959 — [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/).
- 1961: federal probe calls Hot Springs the largest illegal gambling operation in the U.S.; Madden takes the Fifth — [EOA Madden](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/owen-vincent-madden-1702/).
- 1962: closing the gambling became an issue in the governor's race — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). 1964: voters rejected a legalization amendment — [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/); the state took its first decisive steps — [EOA Madden](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/owen-vincent-madden-1702/).
- **conflict:** Rex Nelson writes that Gov. Orval Faubus ended illegal gambling and the last bets were placed on the eve of Easter Sunday 1964 — [NWA / Rex Nelson (snippet)](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2020/jul/12/the-vapors/); EOA, Wikipedia and ADG place the end at Rockefeller's 1967 raids, with the Ohio, Bridge Street, Citizens, White Front and Southern Club operations still seizable in 1967. Reading: a 1964 shutdown that did not hold.
- Motel boom 1950–1965 tied to casinos (Velda Rose Tower Motor Hotel, Park Ave., 1960 or 1963) — [Rex Nelson](https://www.rexnelsonsouthernfried.com/post/along-park-avenue).
- Maxine Temple Jones arrested 1963 — [EoA: Maxine Temple Jones](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/maxine-temple-jones-5338/).

## The 1967 shutdown

- Gov. Winthrop Rockefeller (elected 1966) ordered the State Police crackdown (director Lynn Davis), working with Judge Henry M. Britt — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- The raids came five months after Rockefeller vetoed a bill to legalize gambling at four private clubs — [HMdb marker (via search summary)](https://hmdb.org/m.asp?m=211121).
- Mid-August 1967 raids seized equipment from the Ohio, Bridge Street, Citizens and White Front clubs; an October raid on Southern Club manager Harry Columbus's home took 111 slot machines — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [ADG 2017 via PressReader (via search summary)](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/northwest-arkansas-democrat-gazette/20171007/282406989563864). Exact raid days are approximate ("mid-August").
- Maxine Temple Jones was pardoned by Rockefeller after giving information on illegal gambling — [EoA: Maxine Temple Jones](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/maxine-temple-jones-5338/).
- Oaklawn remained the only legal gambling — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Afterward: the Southern Club's supper-club attempt failed — [EoA: Southern Club](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/southern-club-1208/); the crackdown hurt the motel business — [Rex Nelson](https://www.rexnelsonsouthernfried.com/post/along-park-avenue); the end of illegal gambling is cited (with new medicine) for the bathing decline — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- Legal gaming returned in steps at Oaklawn: simulcasting 1989, instant racing 1999, "electronic games of skill" incl. video poker after a 2005 referendum, a $3M slots/poker/Instant Racing complex 2008 (largest gambling facility in Arkansas), full casino (table games, sports betting, slots) April 1, 2019 under Amendment 100 (approved 2018) — [EOA-OAK](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=4521); [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort).

## Game hooks

All **[inferred]** original mechanics drawn from the notes' hook sections (no borrowed rules or names from existing commercial games).
1. **Two economies (Bathhouse Barons).** Legal squares (bathhouse leases, hotels, racing) vs underground squares (casinos, wire rooms, bookies, slot routes). Underground squares pay a "fine" to the city each round instead of rent/license; a Reform track (1946, 1964–67) can shut them down.
2. **Fines as licenses.** Casino squares pay a fine each round; the fine schedule can rise under a new mayor.
3. **"Go to Grand Jury"** replaces a jail space: the 1947 grand jury (McLaughlin quits but is never convicted).
4. **Election-rigging chance cards:** pink-slip ballots; voters "found only in cemeteries."
5. **GI Revolt minigame:** audit poll-tax receipts, spot alphabetical blocks of 6, 12 or 18 names in one handwriting; a KBI handwriting expert testifies; Judge Miller throws out ~24% (or 1,607 receipts) and the reform slate sweeps.
6. **Mayor's sulky NPC:** McLaughlin rides Central daily behind "Scotch and Soda" with a red carnation (deceased public figure; factual cameo).
7. **"Exiled to the Spa" card:** a rival must never return to their home square (Madden's parole).
8. **Madden marries the postmaster's daughter** and runs the racing wire (event card).
9. **Capone at the Arlington:** a clerk demands he wear a tie (dress-code card); Room 443-vs-442 as a "Myth or Fact" trivia card.
10. **"Luciano Arrested!"** card: roll to see where — Southern Club, the park, or the Arlington — reflecting the disputed accounts.
11. **Prohibition disguise:** the Ohio Club becomes the "Ohio Cigar Store."
12. **Gilded feuds:** Flynn–Doran War (1884) and the police-vs-sheriff gunfight (1899) as event cards.
13. **Faubus relapse (1954):** casinos reopen; the cycle of weekly fines resumes.
14. **Build the Vapors (1960):** Monte Carlo Room, retractable stage, late-night casino; book acts (a crooner rehearses a famous song to a lone bartender — fictionalize).
15. **Bombing events:** Belvedere (1959) and Vapors (Jan. 4, 1963) impose rebuild costs.
16. **Senate rackets hearing (1961):** take the Fifth.
17. **Raid event (Aug. 1967):** State Police seize equipment from four clubs; October raid takes 111 slots; all casino properties pay nothing for 2 turns.
18. **Legal gaming finally on the board:** Oaklawn casino (2019).
19. **Gambling Museum:** restore 80 slot machines as a collectible set.
20. **The Madam's Memoir NPC:** a fictional older NPC writing a tell-all unlocks villager gossip (Maxine archetype; fictionalized).
21. **Gangster Alley property group:** Southern Club, Ohio Club, Gangster Museum (250–510 Central), plus Maxine's.
22. **Icons:** fedora, dice, roulette wheel, spade, neon arrow (avoid real-person likenesses; tommy gun "use sparingly").
